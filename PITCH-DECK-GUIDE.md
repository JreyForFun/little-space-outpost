# Little Space Outpost — PPT Pitch Guide

A ready-to-use outline for a **4–5 minute** hackathon presentation. Keep slide text
short; use the speaker notes to tell the story.

## One-sentence pitch

**Little Space Outpost is a browser game where kids scout a Moon base, build the
systems a crew needs, and make daily choices to keep their first lunar garden alive.**

## Slide 1 — Little Space Outpost

**On the slide**
- *Build. Balance. Protect. Grow.*
- A Moon mission game for young explorers
- Your choices keep Moon Camp going

**Say**
“Imagine you are in charge of a small Moon outpost. The crew wants to grow its
first garden, but every day they have to make choices about the supplies they need
to stay safe.”

**Visual**: Use the game’s Moon Camp success screen or a screenshot of the outpost.
Put the game title and team names here.

**Time**: 15 seconds

## Slide 2 — The challenge

**On the slide**
- Space survival means balancing limited resources.
- Power, air, food, and radiation protection are connected.
- Kids need to experience the trade-offs—not just read about them.

**Say**
“A space mission is not just about building a rocket. A choice that helps one
system can use resources another system needs. We wanted kids to feel that
planning challenge in a simple, playful way.”

**Visual**: Show the four resource labels (air, power, food, shield) around a
simple Moon-base illustration.

**Time**: 30 seconds

## Slide 3 — Meet ASTRO and the crew

**On the slide**
- Help three astronauts find a safe place for Moon Camp.
- Their big goal: grow a tiny Moon garden.
- Explore → Build → Survive

**Say**
“Players join ASTRO and a crew of three. First they scout a landing site, then
they build the outpost, and finally they manage five Moon days while the crew
tries to keep its garden experiment going.”

**Visual**: Show the Explore map and ASTRO’s opening message.

**Time**: 30 seconds

## Slide 4 — Explore and build

**On the slide**
- Scout the map and choose buildable ground.
- Place a habitat, oxygen system, solar panels, shelter, and greenhouse.
- The greenhouse needs power and crew supplies first.

**Say**
“The first stages make planning visible. Players inspect Moon locations, then
choose suitable spots for each structure. For example, solar panels need a sunny
spot, while the shelter needs a more protected one. The garden cannot start until
the outpost has the resources it needs.”

**Visual**: Use one Explore screenshot and one Build screenshot; label the five
structures with small callouts.

**Time**: 35 seconds

## Slide 5 — Every mission day is a trade-off

**On the slide**
- **Grow food:** spend 2 power to grow 3 food.
- **Save power:** gain 2 power, but the crew still uses food.
- **Charge shelter:** spend 2 power to add 2 shields.
- Air and food are used each day; a solar storm may arrive.

**Say**
“In the five-day mission, players choose one plan each day. Growing food costs
power, saving power uses food, and charging the shelter uses power to prepare
for a possible storm. Air and food are consumed as the days pass, so a good plan
has to look ahead.”

**Visual**: Show the mission resource dashboard and the three action cards. Use
arrows to show what each action spends and gains.

**Time**: 45 seconds

## Slide 6 — Grounded in science, honest about the data

**On the slide**
- Radiation and solar activity are real mission-planning concerns.
- NASA/CCMC DONKI is credited as a space-weather reference.
- **This game’s solar alert is randomly generated for practice—not live data.**

**Say**
“The game introduces the real idea that solar activity can create radiation
risks, and it points players to NASA’s DONKI database. To be clear, the storm
event in this prototype is randomized for gameplay; the game does not currently
fetch live DONKI data.”

**Visual**: Show the in-game solar warning and the NASA DONKI name/link. Do not
label the game alert as a real forecast.

**Reference**: [NASA/CCMC DONKI](https://kauai.ccmc.gsfc.nasa.gov/DONKI/)

**Time**: 35 seconds

## Slide 7 — See it in action

**On the slide**
- Scout a safe site.
- Build the outpost.
- Protect the crew and make it through five Moon days.

**Say / demo**
“Here’s the full player journey.” Show a short clip from the pitch video, or
open the game and move quickly through one choice in each stage. Point out the
resource changes after a mission choice, then show the mission ending.

**Visual**: Embed the pitch video or use three screenshots. If presenting live,
have the game loaded before the talk and use the fast-play option for a shorter
demo.

**Time**: 50 seconds

## Slide 8 — The takeaway

**On the slide**
- Space survival is a team-planning problem.
- Choices have consequences—and players can try again.
- **Plan carefully. Protect the crew. Grow the garden.**

**Say**
“We want players to leave with one idea: a Moon outpost works only when its
systems work together. Little Space Outpost turns that idea into choices kids
can try, learn from, and replay.”

**Visual**: Use the “Mission complete!” screen. End with the game URL or a QR
code to your playable build.

**Time**: 30 seconds

## Optional demo checklist

1. Open the game before presenting.
2. Show one safe site selection and one example of a build requirement.
3. In the mission, compare the cost and benefit of two plans.
4. Point out the storm warning and the shelter resource.
5. End on the success or restock screen and explain that replaying lets players
   try another strategy.

## Accuracy notes for your slides

- The game is a learning prototype, not a mission-planning or engineering tool.
- The solar-event day is randomized between mission days 2 and 4.
- NASA DONKI is credited in the game, but the alert is not fetched from DONKI.
- The current prototype does not use OSDR or other live NASA data feeds.
- The “Moon radio: seconds · Mars radio: minutes” line is an educational
  comparison in the game, not a live communications simulation.
